how the system behaves, not a benchmark

A resume is not a track record.

It is a list of things somebody is asking you to believe. AgentR reads each one for whether it is actually backed by something — a project, a scope, a number — before it counts toward anything.

Read in context, not as keywords

A claim attached to a named project, a scope and an outcome is treated differently from the same words sitting alone in a list.

The interview picks up where the claim needs proving

Whatever looks strongest on paper gets asked about directly, so nothing rides on the resume by itself.

How a claim gets judged.

  • proven
    A named project, result or number backing the claim

    “Migrated the platform to a multi-region setup and cut latency by forty percent.” Counts in full.

  • described
    The work is narrated, but nothing on paper backs it

    “Responsible for the platform migration.” Counts, at roughly seventy percent.

  • mentioned
    The word appears and nothing else does

    “Skills: system migrations.” On its own, counts for nothing. This is the line that separates a real track record from a well-worded one.

  • not found
    No evidence, or evidence pointing the other way

    Recorded with high confidence — an absence is a signal too.

What it will not do.

  • It will not contact institutions or employers to formally verify anything. This is a consistency check, not a background check.
  • It will not penalise a thin public footprint on its own. No GitHub or portfolio is not proof of anything.
  • It will not auto-reject anyone. Flags go to a human; nobody is screened out by a rule alone.
  • It will not let vague criteria drive the check. Criteria need to be specific before claims are judged against them.
